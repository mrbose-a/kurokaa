Over time

It gets sharper the longer you run it.

Most software is as useful on day 400 as on day one. Kurokaa isn’t — because everything you record becomes the baseline it measures the next week against.

Week one
Sales, stock movements and the daily cash close are on record. You can already see what sold and whether the till balanced.
Once recipes are filled
Every sale deducts ingredients. Food cost per dish becomes a number instead of an argument.
After a physical count
Stock moves from estimated to verified — and what the system is willing to tell you moves with it.
Over a few weeks
Purchases build a cost history. A supplier raising paneer by ₹18/kg shows up as a margin change, not a surprise at month end.
Every correction you make
Ingredient names you confirm once are remembered. “Lal mirch”, “Kashmiri chilli” and “red chilli powder” stop being three different ingredients.
The founder

Born in the heat of a 10 PM dinner rush.

Kurokaa wasn’t born in a boardroom. It was built in the whites — by a chef who learned to code because the kitchen’s brain was still stuck in the 90s.

Abhishek Bose spent years working the pass as a professional chef before becoming a software engineer. Every kitchen he worked in had the same problem: billing software existed, but nothing actually helped run the restaurant. Reports came three days late, inventory was a guess, and the owner was the integration layer holding it all together.

Kurokaa is the system he wishes he’d had. It is built slowly, with operators, and it runs in a real kitchen every day before it reaches yours.
