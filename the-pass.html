Why it matters

Nobody calls ahead.

Enforcement arrives unannounced — that is the point of it. So the honest question is not are the records in order? It is would you survive an inspection this Tuesday, mid-service, with the fridge you have been meaning to fix?

What it costs

A suspension closes the doors

  • Licences suspended under section 32 of the Food Safety and Standards Act
  • Spot fines levied and stock seized on the day
  • Shutters down, staff idle, rent still due
What it costs

The reputation outlasts the fix

  • The repair takes a week
  • The photo of your shutter with a notice on it lasts as long as the internet
  • Regulars find somewhere else to eat and stay there
What it costs

The failure is never exotic

  • A fridge nobody probed
  • A water test nobody booked
  • A record nobody signed — cheap to fix, invisible from the inside

Most kitchens genuinely do not know where they stand. Not because anyone is careless — because nobody has walked the room with a written standard in hand and said so. The chef is too close to it, the owner is looking at the P&L, and the first person to look properly is usually holding a notice pad.

What lands in your inbox

A report in 72 hours

  • A plain answer to whether this kitchen would withstand an inspection today
  • Every finding graded critical, major, minor or observation, each with a deadline
  • Dated photographs of what was found
  • A thirty-day plan ordered by what is cheapest and fastest to put right
  • What we could not assess, and why — a report claiming to have seen everything is exaggerating
What happens to what we see

Where all of it goes

  • The report goes to you. Not a regulator, landlord or insurer — unless you ask us in writing
  • Photos are of findings, not people. A drain or a shelf, never a member of your team at work
  • Your staff are never named. Nothing anyone says is attributed
  • No case studies without your written permission, asked for afterwards and easy to refuse
  • We keep the file two years so a re-visit has something to compare against, then delete it

The one thing we reserve is the right to end the engagement if something critical is found and simply left. We are not inspectors and we will not report you to anyone — but we will not stay on as the name attached to a kitchen that has decided to ignore us.

How we judge

Every limit says where it came from.

A consultant's number that quietly becomes “an FSSAI requirement” because it once appeared in a report is how people get misled without anyone deciding to mislead them. So every control we check carries its own source.

Source 01

Regulatory reference

  • Traceable to a named Indian instrument
  • The Food Safety and Standards Act, or Schedule 4 of the 2011 Regulations
  • Cited in the report, re-checked annually
Source 02

Operational default

  • Our own working rule, drawn from UK kitchen practice
  • Marked as ours, carrying no regulatory claim at all
  • Most of the control set sits here, honestly
Source 03

Your own standard

  • A rule stricter than ours that you choose to adopt
  • Set with you during the review
  • Never imposed
